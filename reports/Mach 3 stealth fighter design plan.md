# Trade Mach 3 Before Drawing This Stealth Fighter

Every real fighter program, whether Lockheed Martin, Sukhoi, MiG or KAI, goes through the same sequence before any metal is cut. First comes a requirements set with threshold and objective values. Conceptual design ends when one configuration is selected. Preliminary design ends in a lofted, weighed, frozen configuration at the preliminary design review (PDR). Detail design ends in drawing release at the critical design review (CDR). Tens of thousands of wind-tunnel hours and a body of airworthiness, safety and technology-maturity evidence support each step. With free tools, a two-person team can reproduce the digital content of the first two and a half of those steps in about three years. It cannot produce a CDR-level, test-validated fighter. The re-runnable entry analysis behind this report shows why the team's first job is the requirements, not the drawings. Mach 3 costs little in thrust once the engine is a high-throttle-ratio turbojet or turbo-ramjet. It does, however, heat the skin to about **220–270 °C** (recovery temperature **291–301 °C** at 20–24 km) and multiply mid-wave infrared skin emission about **1,000-fold** over Mach 0.9 cruise. An interceptor that must also sustain **5 g at Mach 0.9** sizes to about **43.5 t with two 221 kN engines**. The design also sits on a **sizing cliff**: every extra 0.1 in the empty-weight factor for stealth or hot structure roughly doubles takeoff weight, and an F-22-calibrated penalty does not close at any practical size. The recommended baseline is therefore a fighter of about **16–19 t** (28–34 t with a 10% weight penalty) with these requirements: a 100 km Mach 3 dash, Mach 1.6 supercruise, and 4 g sustained turning. It has a chined, blended planform, titanium hot structure, and a variable 2-D mixed-compression inlet hidden under the forebody. "Invisible" is redefined as frontal radar shaping plus short, high, fast exposure, the A-12 approach, accepting that infrared and VHF radar will detect the aircraft during the dash. All tonnages are first-pass (±25–30%) and rest on several textbook coefficients that could not be re-verified. The most important number to estimate next, the mass penalty of a hot low-observable structure, has no public source at all.

## Every program clears the same gates before cutting metal

### Four phases, a ladder of reviews, one requirements document

Design textbooks divide aircraft design into **conceptual, preliminary and detail design** ([engrXiv](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/view/4286/version/5841/7448)). Conceptual design takes "the vague idea of an aircraft", works up alternatives and narrows them to one configuration ([Project Raven](https://projectraven.substack.com/p/conceptual-design-part-2-towards)). In Raymer's flow chart, preliminary design starts when a **"single concept [is] selected"**, and its goal is to finalise weight and performance estimates ([Raymer, aircraftdesign.com](https://aircraftdesign.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/HowRaymerWroteADACA.pdf)). Detail design covers production design and ends in manufacturing. Requirements definition comes before all three. Ilyushin engineers argue that contradictions and "excessive requirements" in the customer's tactical-technical assignment (TTZ) must be caught at the technical-proposal and avanproekt stages, before they become program failures ([Vestnik SSAU](https://journals.ssau.ru/vestnik/article/download/2288/2309)). That warning applies directly to a brief that asks for Mach 3 and stealth together.

The US Department of Defense (DoD) wraps the same logic in milestones and systems-engineering technical reviews. Milestone A approves entry into Technology Maturation and Risk Reduction (TMRR) and sets that phase's exit criteria ([DAU](https://aaf.dau.edu/mca/milestone-a/)). Unless waived, **a PDR is held before Milestone B** ([DAU](https://aaf.dau.edu/mca/milestone-b/)). The reviews "establish the technical baselines, assess the system's technical maturity, and review and assess technical risks" ([DAU](https://aaf.dau.edu/aaf/mca/tech-reviews/)). They are held when the entrance criteria written into the Systems Engineering Plan are met, not on calendar dates ([AcqNotes](https://acqnotes.com/acqnote/careerfields/se-technical-reviews-and-audits)). The ladder runs as follows. The alternative systems review (ASR) follows the concept studies once a preferred concept is chosen ([DAU SE Fundamentals](https://cdn.ez-pdh.com/course-material/Systems-Engineering-Fundamentals-Part-2.pdf)). The system requirements review (SRR) confirms the developer understands the requirements ([AcqNotes](https://acqnotes.com/acqNote/system-requirements-reviewsrrse)). The system functional review (SFR) lowers the risk of proceeding to PDR ([DAU](https://aaf.dau.edu/aaf/mca/sfr)). PDR works at the allocated baseline ([NDIA](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2009/systemengr/8883WednesdayTrack4Eischens.pdf)). CDR approves the build-to design. Korea's KF-21 CDR (24–26 Sept 2019) reviewed about **390 technical data items** and cleared prototype production ([FlightGlobal](https://www.flightglobal.com/k-fx-cdr-sets-stage-for-prototype-production/134497.article)). The formal framework is changing. JCIDS was terminated by memos in August and November 2025 ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/08/pentagon-terminating-jcids-process-as-part-of-larger-acquisition-reform-memo/); [DoD memo](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Nov/10/2003819442/-1/-1/1/REFORMING-THE-JOINT-REQUIREMENTS-PROCESS-TO-ACCELERATE-FIELDING-OF-WARFIGHTING-CAPABILITIES.PDF)). Program executive offices are being regrouped into Portfolio Acquisition Executives ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/war-secretary-announces-strategy-regarding-defense-acquisition-reform)). DAU logged a Change 2 to the acquisition instruction on 8 April 2026 ([DAU change log](https://aaf.dau.edu/aaf/policies-change-log/)). The review sequence nonetheless remains the engineering reference model.

Russian practice maps onto this almost one to one. Teaching and professional standards list the **technical proposal, avanproekt, eskizny proekt, maket and technical project** ([NSTU syllabus](https://www.nstu.ru/studies/study/edu_plans/disc_list/disc_program/download_wp?id=81848); [ppt.ru professional standard](https://ppt.ru/docs/profstandarts/details/90), marked as no longer in force). The step with no US equivalent is the **maket (full-scale mock-up) commission**, a customer gate before prototype construction. For the Yak-152 it was held in September 2014 (press release relayed by a forum, so it needs verifying) ([DCS forum](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/110750-jak-152/)). Historically, chief designers sent plans and models to TsAGI for inspection and structural breaking tests ([CIA reading room](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/node/947703)). Sukhoi's PAK FA timeline shows the pace: tender in April 2002, **exterior design approved 10 December 2004**, first flight 29 January 2010, service entry in December 2020 ([Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/?p=14874); [GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/russia/pak-fa-program.htm)).

The ATF shows how fighter requirements are actually built and traded. The USAF issued an RFI in 1981, and seven companies answered with 19 concepts. By late 1984 the requirements had settled on **50,000 lb takeoff weight, an 800 mi radius, supercruise at Mach 1.4–1.5 and a 2,000 ft runway**. Stealth requirements were then "drastically increased in December 1985" ([Wikipedia, ATF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Tactical_Fighter)). Dem/Val was used to decide what capability fit within a **$35 million flyaway-cost goal** ([GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems//aircraft/f-22-cost.htm)). Even the winner was picked partly because the Air Force "had more confidence in Lockheed and Pratt & Whitney to 'manage' the program" ([AFMC History](https://www.afsc.af.mil/News/Article/2919531/flashback-northrop-yf-23-black-widow-ii/)). When stealth was raised sharply, speed stayed at **Mach 1.4–1.5 supercruise**. No precedent in this research pairs Mach 3 with radar and IR low observability as fixed, co-equal requirements.

### Recovery temperatures of 291–349 °C rule out aluminium and epoxy

The adiabatic-wall temperature is T_aw = T∞[1 + r(γ−1)M²/2], with a turbulent recovery factor r = Pr^(1/3) ≈ 0.89–0.896 ([pygasflow](https://pygasflow.readthedocs.io/en/latest/modules/atd/temperatures.html)). Skin equilibrium comes from h(T_aw − T_w) = εσ(T_w⁴ − T∞⁴), using Eckert reference-temperature convection on a turbulent flat plate (calc: `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/out/step1_atmos_thermal.md`).

| Condition | Stagnation T0 (°C) | T_aw, r = 0.89 (°C) | Skin at x = 5 m, ε = 0.9 (°C) | Skin, ε = 0.3 (°C) | Dynamic pressure q (kPa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M0.9, 11 km | −21 | −25 | −26 | −25 | – |
| M2.83, 20 km (MiG-25 limit) | 291 | 252 | 208 | 234 | 31.0 |
| **M3.0, 20 km** | 333 | **291** | **236** | 267 | **34.8** |
| M3.0, 24 km | 344 | 301 | 221 | 262 | 18.7 |
| M3.2, 24 km | 399 | 349 | 250 | 299 | 21.3 |
| M3.0, 11 km (for contrast) | 334 | 291 | – | – | 143.0 |

The model checks out against the Blackbird. Measured SR-71 average surface temperatures at speed were **462–622 °F (≈240–330 °C)** ([Merlin, NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20090007797/downloads/20090007797.pdf)). NASA quotes "up to 800 °F" ([NASA FS-030](https://www.nasa.gov/centers/dryden/pdf/495839main_FS-030_SR-71.pdf)). Kelly Johnson's design temperatures were 585 °F at the nose, 470 °F at the tail and 1,050 °F at the nacelle, the last driven by engine heat ([Code One](https://www.codeonemagazine.com/article.html?item_id=153)). The computed Mach 3.2 / 24 km skin of 250–265 °C falls inside that band. The material consequences are direct. 2618-T61 aluminium keeps only about 80% of its properties at 400 °F, and only after short exposure ([DTIC AD0801499](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/AD0801499/index.html)). Ti-6Al-4V is rated for service through **750 °F (400 °C)** ([Rolled Alloys](https://www.rolledalloys.com/wp-content/uploads/6AL4V_Data-sheet.pdf)). PMR-15 polyimide composites top out at **260–288 °C** ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20050187016)). Inconel 718 is good to about 650 °C ([Xometry](https://xometry.pro/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Inconel-718-1.pdf)). Mach 3 therefore means a titanium hot structure with superalloy nacelles, polyimides only on shaded or cooled surfaces, and radar absorbers that survive 250–350 °C. The F-22's 24% thermoset-composite share ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20040086015/downloads/20040086015.pdf)) does not carry over.

A "dash-only" requirement does not avoid hot skins. Lumped heat-soak time constants at Mach 3 / 20 km are **40 s for 1.5 mm titanium** and 49–56 s for steel, aluminium or composite skins. A 100 km dash lasts 113 s and reaches 87–94% of skin equilibrium. What the dash limits is soak into thick structure, fuel and avionics. Dynamic pressure gives the other envelope rule: Mach 3 at 11 km means 143 kPa, against 35 kPa at 20 km, so Mach 3 belongs at 18 km and above. Two thermal-structural rules of thumb, both using recalled property values to be confirmed in MMPDS, set the sizing cases. Fully restrained titanium sees about σ = EαΔT/(1−ν) ≈ 150 MPa per 100 K of skin-to-spar temperature difference. A 20 m titanium airframe grows about 6 cm at ΔT ≈ 350 K, consistent with the SR-71's widely reported 3–4 inch growth.

### A Mach 3 skin glows about a thousand times brighter in mid-wave infrared

Planck-band integration of the equilibrium skins gives the infrared penalty (calc: `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/out/step1_atmos_thermal.md`).

| Case (x = 5 m) | ε | Skin T (K) | Total exitance (W/m²) | 3–5 µm (W/m²) | 8–12 µm (W/m²) | Ratio vs M0.9: total / MWIR / LWIR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M0.9, 11 km | 0.9 | 247 | 191 | 0.5 | 38.7 | 1 / 1 / 1 |
| M1.6, 13 km | 0.9 | 312 | 486 | 8.1 | 132 | 2.5 / 15 / 3.4 |
| M2.83, 20 km | 0.9 | 482 | 2,746 | 363 | 727 | 14 / 678 / 19 |
| **M3.0, 20 km** | 0.9 | 509 | **3,428** | **537** | **867** | **18 / ~1,000 / 22** |
| M3.0, 20 km | 0.3 | 540 | 1,445 | 266 | 345 | 7.6 / 497 / 8.9 |
| M3.2, 24 km | 0.9 | 523 | 3,825 | 648 | 943 | 20 / 1,210 / 24 |

The low-emissivity option is a trap. Cutting ε from 0.9 to 0.3 raises the skin temperature by 31 K and only halves in-band MWIR. The signature-reduction note found that matching a 30 °C skin would need **ε ≈ 0.005 in MWIR**, which no practical coating achieves. Low ε also costs radiative cooling, which at 573 K and ε = 0.9 removes about 5.5 kW/m². For a point-source IRST, detection range grows as the square root of in-band intensity contrast, not the fourth root that applies to radar. Skin heating alone therefore stretches MWIR detection range by roughly **14×** on a 30 °C → 300 °C basis, and more on a like-for-like stratospheric comparison. Because a subsonic fighter's MWIR signature is dominated by plume and hot parts, these skin-only ratios overstate the total-signature penalty from some aspects. The direction is not in doubt. A KAIST study found that skin temperatures above about 300 K at supersonic cruise make skin IR suppression a key survivability item ([KAIST/JSASS](https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/101853)). By Wien's law (λ_peak ≈ 2,898 µm·K / T), the emission peak moves from about 9.6 µm for a 30 °C skin to about 5.1–5.7 µm for 509–564 K Mach 3 skins, at the long edge of the 3–5 µm band that IR seekers use.

### Conventional turbofans run out of compressor near Mach 2.2

Ram compression heats the compressor face to **333 °C at Mach 3.0 and 387 °C at Mach 3.2**, and no inlet efficiency changes that. If compressor-exit temperature is capped at 950 K, the ideal maximum compressor pressure ratio π_c,max = (T_t3,max/T_t2)^3.5 falls from 65 at sea-level static to 22.6 at Mach 2, **4.8 at Mach 3** and 3.6 at Mach 3.2. The MiG-25's R-15B-300 had an overall pressure ratio of exactly **4.75** ([Wikipedia, R-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumansky_R-15)) and a life of only **150 h** ([ArmedConflicts](https://www.armedconflicts.com/Mikulin-R-15B-300-t176038)). A fifth-generation-class pressure ratio of 25 would push compressor-exit temperature to about 1,522 K at Mach 3, which is impossible. For an ideal turbojet at Mach 3 the optimum compressor ratio is about 1.1. The afterburning low-pressure-ratio turbojet and the ideal ramjet converge to about **800–900 N·s/kg** specific thrust and 1.5–1.65/h ideal TSFC (calc: `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/propulsion_cycle_calc.py`). The J58 shows the same physics in hardware. At Mach 3+ the inlet supplied about **54%** of net thrust, the ejector about **29%** and the turbomachinery only about **17%** ([Enginehistory.org](https://enginehistory.org/Convention/2013/SR-71Propul/SR-71Propul.shtml), snippet only). The MiG-25 confirms where the limit bites: its Mach 2.83 service limit was set by the engines, and Mach 3.2 dashes wrecked them ([MiGFlug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/mig-25-foxbat/); [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2018/09/06/the-daring-defection-of-the-secret-soviet-super-fighter/)).

Mattingly's installed-thrust lapse fits [T] show the cliff in constraint terms. For a low-bypass mixed-flow turbofan with throttle ratio 1.07 (F119/F100 class, engine P1), the maximum-power lapse α is 0.25 at Mach 2 / 15 km and **zero from about Mach 2.2 upward**. A high-throttle-ratio afterburning turbojet (engine P2) designed to reach its turbine-temperature limit at Mach 3 cruise (throttle ratio 2.105, the J58 design intent) has α = 0.99 at Mach 3 / 20 km. Its TSFC there is 1.90/h, which matches the cruise figure sometimes quoted for the J58 (unverified) (calc: `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/out/step2_constraints.md`). Above about Mach 2.8 the fit's dry branch exceeds its maximum branch (1.16 vs 0.99 at Mach 3 / 20 km), so the study uses the afterburning branch for all Mach numbers from 2.5 up. That is J58 practice, but it also means a continuous plume. The only sourced engine TSFC in the notes is the MiG-31's D-30F6 at **0.716 dry / 1.863 afterburning** per hour ([Wikipedia, TSFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-specific_fuel_consumption)). Its thrust-to-weight of about 6.4 (152 kN, 2,416 kg) ([Wikipedia, D-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soloviev_D-30)) anchors the study's assumed engine T/W of 6 for P2. No adaptive engine is publicly Mach 3-capable. NGAP prototypes reached digital assembly-readiness reviews in May 2026, then reportedly slipped another year ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/05/air-force-sees-another-year-delay-for-next-gen-engines/)).

Mach 3 cruise is not thermodynamically wasteful. Overall efficiency η0 = V0/(TSFC·h_PR) reaches about 0.4–0.5 at Mach 3, against about 0.24 at Mach 0.85. The range penalty comes instead from low lift-to-drag ratio and from the climb. A 25 t fighter at L/D 4.5 and TSFC 1.9/h burns about **10,600 kg/h** at Mach 3. All of that fuel is the aircraft's only heat sink, because ram air arrives at 333 °C. Standard JP-8 absorbs only about 270 kJ/kg up to its **163 °C** bulk limit, while JP-8+100 is rated to 218 °C bulk ([SAE 932084](https://saemobilus.sae.org/content/932084)). The AFRL program aimed to raise JP-8 from 325 °F to 550 °F for "120 percent higher heat sink" ([National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11780/chapter/8)). The SR-71's JP-7 reached the burners at about 316 °C after cooling the cockpit, avionics and hydraulics ([Merlin](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20090007797/downloads/20090007797.pdf)). At 2.9 kg/s, JP-8 gives about 0.8 MW of cooling and a JP-8+100-class fuel about 1.7 MW, and that capacity collapses at low throttle. The F-35's roughly 30 kW cooling system ([Defense Daily](https://defensedaily.com/replacing-f-35-ptms-may-cost-3-billion-honeywell-estimates/air-force)), which draws more engine bleed than planned, shows the cost of under-sizing thermal management.

### The subsonic 5 g turn, not Mach 3, sets thrust and wing size

Constraint analysis uses Mattingly's master equation [T]:

T_SL/W_TO = (β/α){(qS/βW_TO)[K1(nβW_TO/qS)² + K2(nβW_TO/qS) + C_D0] + P_s/V}

The drag polar has C_D0(M) rising from 0.014 subsonic to a 0.028 peak at Mach 1.2 and falling to 0.0213 at Mach 3. That table is a low-confidence assumption above Mach 2, since no measured supersonic C_D0 was found. K1 = 0.18 subsonic, blended into Raymer's no-suction supersonic formula, gives (L/D)max of 9.96 subsonic, 5.72 at Mach 2 and **4.90 at Mach 3**. The sourced upper references are 6.6 for the A-12/SR-71 family and 7.4 for Concorde ([Mason, Virginia Tech](https://archive.aoe.vt.edu/mason/Mason_f/SupersonicPres.pdf)).

With the Mach 3-capable engine, Mach 3 cruise needs only T/W of about 0.2–0.45 and **is never the thrust driver**. The 5 g sustained turn and the 120 m/s specific-excess-power requirement at Mach 0.9 / 9 km set the design. The minimum-T/W point is W/S ≈ 2,200 N/m² at T/W 1.02, and minimum takeoff weight selects **W/S 2,300 N/m² at T/W 1.035**. Landing in 900 m caps W/S at 4,407 N/m². Relaxing to 4 g moves the optimum to **W/S ≈ 3,550 N/m² at T/W ≈ 0.94**. Run with an F119-class engine and no Mach 3 requirement, the same constraint set lands at W/S 3,250 N/m² and T/W 1.20, close to the F-22's 3,642 N/m² and 1.10. That agreement suggests the constraint set is realistic (plots: `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/out/constraint_diagram_P2_selected.png` and `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/out/constraint_diagram_P1.png`). The low wing loading creates a second conflict. Best Mach 3 L/D wants C_L ≈ 0.21, which at W/S 3,000 puts the optimum altitude at 27.2 km. Under a 25 km ceiling the selected W/S 2,300 design cruises at L/D 4.2 instead of 4.9. The Russian speed references sit far from the stealth-fighter corner of the diagram: MiG-25P at 5,865 N/m² and T/W 0.56, MiG-31 at 6,527 and 0.76, SR-71A at 3,936 and 0.44.

### Weight closes at 43.5 t, then falls off a cliff

Three missions were sized with 900 kg of internal payload, a 100 kg pilot and MIL-C-5011A-style reserves (20 min sea-level loiter, plus 5% of mission fuel, plus 1% trapped fuel). **Profile A** is an interceptor flying 500 km out at Mach 3 and 500 km back at Mach 2.5, with 2 min of combat at Mach 1.5. **A-dash** flies 400 km at Mach 0.9, then a 100 km Mach 3 dash, combat, and 500 km back at Mach 0.9. **Profile B** is a strike/air-superiority mission with 600 km at Mach 0.9 plus 200 km of Mach 1.6 supercruise each way (800 km radius). The solver bisects W0 = We(W0) + Wf(W0) + W_crew + W_payload, which is the closure W0 = (W_crew + W_payload)/(1 − Wf/W0 − We/W0). Mission legs use Breguet, W_i/W_{i−1} = exp[−R·c/(V·L/D)]. The empty-weight factor K_we multiplies the Raymer fit and carries any stealth or hot-structure penalty. An explicit engine-mass correction ΔWe = (T/W − 1)·W0/(T/W)_engine was added because Raymer's (T/W)^0.04 term barely penalises big engines (calc: `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/out/step3_sizing.md`).

## Seven ways Mach 3 undoes low observability

The A-12 is the only historical precedent for a Mach 3 aircraft designed with radar cross-section (RCS) in mind, and it set the pattern. A 1958 CIA panel judged that a much faster, higher-flying aircraft could be made nearly undetectable and that speed greatly reduced the chance of detection, while absorbent-material work "helped but did not solve the problem" ([Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/1194oxcart/)). Production A-12s carried a coating of ferrite particles in a plastic binder. When the Soviet TALL KING radar appeared, the chines were rebuilt at a cost the CIA history calls expensive and "undesirable", with reduced performance ([CIA, Archangel](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Archangel-CIAs-Supersonic-A-12-Reconnaissance-Aircraft.pdf)). Kelly Johnson proposed a cesium fuel additive to cut the afterburner's aft-quadrant radar return ([The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/29787/the-sr-71-blackbirds-predecessor-created-plasma-stealth-by-burning-cesium-laced-fuel.md)). In other words, speed and altitude were the primary defence and RCS reduction a partial supplement. The conflicts below show why.

| Conflict | Mach 3 effect (numbers) | Why it hurts low observability | Mitigation direction |

Two further penalties have no LO mitigation at all. The first is a continuous sonic-boom carpet: the Mach angle is 19.5° at Mach 3. The second is maintenance. The F-22 needs about **34 maintenance hours per flight hour** against 19 for the F-16, mostly for RAM ([StrategyPage](https://www.strategypage.com/htmw/htcbtsp/20110410.aspx)). About half of the B-2's 48 hours per flight hour went to LO features ([AIN](https://ainonline.com/aviation-news/defense/2006-11-30/usaf-counts-true-cost-b-2-stealth)). A −55 °C to +300 °C cycle every sortie would make that worse. The canopy and escape system add a cockpit-level version of the same conflict. The SR-71 windshield ran at about 620 °F and used quartz (secondary source) ([Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/how-sr-71-blackbird-flew-so-fast-windshield-hot-cook-food/)), while a stealth canopy needs a conductive RF film rated to that temperature, with no open data. The radar arithmetic is unforgiving in its own way. Detection range scales as σ^¼. Against a radar that sees 1 m² at 200 km, an F-15-class 10 m² target is seen at 356 km and a "marble" 0.0001 m² target at 20 km. A 10× range cut costs 40 dB, so a Mach 3 design that loses even 10 dB of frontal RCS to inlet geometry gives back about 78% more detection range (10^0.25 ≈ 1.78). Every public RCS figure is unofficial, and the "marble" and "golf ball" values are simply metal-sphere conversions of a metaphor ([GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/stealth-aircraft-rcs.htm); [StrategyPage](https://www.strategypage.com/htmw/htairfo/articles/20051125.aspx)).

## Recommended baseline: a Mach 3 dash fighter with a 4 g wing

The entry analysis gives a clear ranking: the subsonic turn requirement, Mach 3 time and distance, and the empty-weight penalty dominate everything else. The recommended requirement set below keeps all three of the brief's goals in recognisable form but makes each a threshold/objective pair, as the ATF did with cost. The resulting **baseline concept** sizes at **16.0 t (A-dash) to 18.8 t (strike mission B)** at K_we = 1.0, or **28–34 t** at K_we = 1.1. It has W/S ≈ 3,600 N/m², T/W ≈ 0.95, a wing of about 44–51 m² (76–94 m² at K_we = 1.1) and 149–175 kN of total sea-level afterburning thrust (261–320 kN at K_we = 1.1). The configuration is a chined, blended body. On the SR-71, chine lift rose roughly with Mach squared and supplied **17–20% of total lift**, offsetting the aft shift of wing lift ([Wikipedia, Chine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chine_(aeronautics))). Combined with fuel transfer to move the centre of gravity aft during acceleration, it removed the need for canards or special trim devices ([CIA, flight characteristics](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/flight%20characteristics%5B15271431%5D.pdf)). The planform is a cranked or trapezoidal wing with 55–65° edge-aligned leading edges, thin (3–4%) sections and aspect ratio about 2.5. It uses canted twin tails, since fin effectiveness at Mach 3 is about 0.61 of its Mach 2 value and a tailless layout would need very strong yaw control there. Propulsion is a twin "rubber" high-throttle-ratio afterburning turbojet or turbo-ramjet fed by 2-D variable mixed-compression inlets under the chine. A single-engine variant becomes conceivable at the 4 g / dash requirement set (about 149 kN total) and should be traded at Gate 2. It would avoid the asymmetric-unstart yaw behind the SR-71 61-7952 breakup at Mach 3.18 ([Aviation Safety Network](https://cdn.aviation-safety.net/wikibase/162372)) but gives up engine-out redundancy. Nozzles should be 2-D convergent-divergent with variable expansion and secondary-air cooling, traded against an ejector. The F119's ±20° pitch vectoring ([Pratt & Whitney](https://www.rtx.com/prattwhitney/products/military-engines/f119)) is the LO-compatible precedent.

Inside the aircraft, the structure is titanium acreage (Ti-6Al-4V and beta titanium) with nickel-alloy nacelles. Ceramic radar-absorbing structure goes on leading edges, chines and inlet lips. Expansion is accommodated under the outer skin rather than through SR-71-style loose panels. Fuel is at least a JP-8+100-class high-heat-sink fuel, with nitrogen inerting as on the XB-70 ([FIU](https://web.eng.fiu.edu/allstar/wings7.htm)). The control and avionics architecture follows the F-35: three distributed vehicle-management computers, electro-hydrostatic actuators (whose heat rejection in a hot airframe is an open risk), and NDI or incremental NDI (INDI) laws with control allocation. INDI has flown on a certified aircraft and tolerates aero-model error better than NDI ([Grondman et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322308968_Design_and_Flight_Testing_of_Incremental_Nonlinear_Dynamic_Inversion-based_Control_Laws_for_a_Passenger_Aircraft)). Propulsion and flight control are integrated, with unstart detection and Mach-scheduled fuel-transfer CG management. A flight-critical DAL A domain is kept separate from a MOSA mission domain. The single-seat cockpit uses a helmet display, DAS-style IR apertures to shrink the hot canopy, and an AI assistant consistent with X-62A machine-learning flight ([CSIAC](https://csiac.dtic.mil/articles/dod-artificial-intelligence-agents-successfully-pilot-fighter-jet/)) and CCA teaming, now in production ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/industry/techwatch/2026/06/18/us-air-force-awards-first-cca-production-contracts-to-general-atomics-anduril/)). Life support is a full pressure suit, because cruise sits well above the **~63,000 ft Armstrong limit** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_limit)). Escape is an advanced open seat (US16E 600 KEAS / 50,000 ft ([Martin-Baker](https://martin-baker.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/US16E-2026-Data-Sheet.pdf)); K-36D 0–755 KEAS ([DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA321294.pdf))) plus that suit, traded against a capsule. Mach 3 at 22–24 km corresponds to about 340–400 KEAS (from q = 18.7–25.5 kPa in the calc tables; the flight-controls note's 400–450 KEAS estimate looks high). That is inside seat speed ratings but above their altitude ceilings, which is the SR-71 solution (its seat was qualified to about 450 KEAS ([NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19800015008/downloads/19800015008.pdf))).

## A 36-month, six-gate plan for two engineers

The plan reproduces the content of the lifecycle above at study fidelity. It aims at a **PDR-level digital baseline plus a selected-part detail package**: 3D master model, general-arrangement and inboard-profile drawings, structural arrangement, full calculation set, and model-based definition (MBD) for representative parts. A full CDR drawing set is out of scope. Four principles run through every phase:

- One parametric master geometry that every analysis references.
- One assumption register, grown from `research_notes/Mach 3 stealth fighter design plan/calc/assumptions.py`, with V/T/A/L confidence codes.
- Requirement IDs traced to the analyses and parts that satisfy them.
- Event-driven gates with written entry and exit criteria, reviewed by at least one outside peer.

Roles split naturally into an "outer loop" engineer (requirements, aerodynamics, propulsion, performance, signatures) and an "inner loop" engineer (geometry and CAD, structures and thermal, weights, systems, flight dynamics and controls, cockpit). Durations are this report's planning judgement for two full-time people: no open source gives person-hours by phase. For scale, Sukhoi needed about 2.7 years with a full design bureau to reach an approved exterior shape.

| Phase | Months | Purpose | Main deliverables | Gate (analogue) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Workbench | 0–2 | Legal, reproducible, validated toolchain and data | Project/SE plan, tool validation report, verified assumption register v1, legal memo | Gate 0: toolchain and data readiness |
| 1. Requirements | 2–5 | Mission, threat, threshold/objective requirements that close | System requirements document, ConOps, sizing trade report, hazard log v0, verification matrix skeleton | Gate 1 (SRR) |
| 2. Concept | 5–11 | 3–5 layouts, trade studies, down-select | Trade report, selected three-view and inboard profile, Class II weights, first engine decks, PO RCS polars | Gate 2 (ASR/SFR) |
| 3. Configuration | 11–18 | Lofted OML, CFD aero database, engine deck, signature and thermal maps | Frozen OML v1.0, aero DB v1, engine deck v1, signature report v1 | Gate 3 (configuration freeze; Su-57 "exterior approved") |
| 4. Preliminary design | 18–28 | Structure, subsystems, flight dynamics, controls, cockpit, escape | PDR data package (allocated baseline) | Gate 4 (PDR) |
| 5. Detail and blueprints | 28–36 | Selected-part MBD, drawings, interface control documents (ICDs), weight and balance, virtual maket | Blueprint package, 3D master model, final report | Gate 5 (CDR-lite / maket commission) |

### Phase 0, months 0–2: build a workbench that can be trusted

The first task is a short legal memo. If the team is in Poland, as the research brief assumed, transfers abroad of controlled goods or technology, including by email or cloud, fall under the Act of 29 November 2000. Its consolidated text is Dz.U. 2023 poz. 1582 ([ELI](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2023/1582/ogl/pol/pdf)), amended in 2026 ([Sejm](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026/471/text.pdf)). The EU Common Military List puts aircraft under ML10 and their technology under ML22 ([Directive 2009/43/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2009/0043?view=plain)). Dual-use rules cover electronic transfer ([BMWET](https://www.bmwet.gv.at/en/Topics/Export-Control/Export/Dual-Use.html)). Public-domain information is carved out under ITAR 22 CFR 120.34 ([Stony Brook](https://www.stonybrook.edu/research-security/export-controls/reference_guides/itar.html)). This is information, not legal advice. The team should use only published sources and DTIC "Distribution A" documents, and keep its outputs in-country until counsel clears publication.

Next comes the repository: Git plus DVC 3.67.1 for large CFD and FEA data ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dvc/)), one directory per discipline, and the existing calc scripts as the seed. Every tool is then validated on a closed-form or published case before it touches the design:

- OpenVSP's wave-drag tool against the Sears–Haack result D/q = (9π/2)(A_max/l)² [T].
- POFACETS against a flat plate (σ = 4πA²/λ²), a dihedral and a sphere [Knott formulas, to verify].
- SU2 on the supersonic cases used in a Calgary verification thesis ([U Calgary](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/113512)).
- JSBSim or the open Stevens & Lewis F-16 model ([GitHub](https://github.com/EthanJamesLew/f16-flight-dynamics)) against the textbook's trim results ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Aircraft+Control+and+Simulation:+Dynamics,+Controls+Design,+and+Autonomous+Systems,+3rd+Edition-p-9781118870983)).
- pyCycle's afterburning-turbojet and mixed-flow-turbofan examples ([GitHub](https://github.com/OpenMDAO/pyCycle)).

The team also buys or borrows the core texts: Raymer, Nicolai & Carichner, Mattingly et al. ([Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Aircraft_Engine_Design.html?id=2Wy5rpdm3DMC)), Seddon & Goldsmith, Knott, Stevens & Lewis and Niu (editions to confirm). With the books in hand, it closes the top items of the verification table at the end of this report, starting with Raymer Table 6.1 and Mattingly's lapse and TSFC coefficients. **Gate 0 exit:** `run_all.py` reproduces; every [T] coefficient is either confirmed in the book or replaced; canonical validation cases agree with closed forms (suggested tolerance: within 5% for wave drag and 1 dB for RCS); the legal memo is filed.

### Phase 1, months 2–5: write requirements that close

This phase writes the concept of operations (ConOps). It names the threat (VHF early warning, IRST, long-range surface-to-air missiles) and the reason for Mach 3, a time-critical intercept or a kinematic advantage for missiles. The US record shows that Mach 3 departs from doctrine and has to be justified explicitly. Profiles A, A-dash and B and their variants become mission definitions. Each requirement gets an ID, a threshold, an objective and a verification method: analysis, inspection, demonstration or test. Signature requirements are stated per band, aspect sector, polarisation and statistic, because single RCS numbers are meaningless. The requirements live in Eclipse Capella, scripted through capellambse 0.8.1 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/capellambse/)), or in SysML v2 if the team prefers. The trade-study template is fixed now: objective, alternatives, criteria weighted and tied to requirement IDs, method and data, scoring with sensitivity, recommendation, and decision record. Constraint and sizing analysis is re-run with the verified coefficients, and the energy-state climb (d ln W = −c·T·dh_e/[V(T − D)], P_s = V(T − D)/W) replaces the Raymer fit. Sizing can stay in the Python scripts or move to OpenMDAO 3.45.2 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/openmdao/)) with AeroSandbox 4.2.10 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/aerosandbox/)), and trajectories to Dymos 1.15.1 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/dymos/)). NASA Aviary 1.0.1 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/aviary/)) and FAST-OAD lean toward transport aircraft. The phase also seeds a MIL-STD-882E hazard log, which ranks each hazard by severity (1–4) and probability (A–F). Finally it drafts a verification matrix mapped to MIL-HDBK-516C sections 6 (Flight Technology), 9 (Crew Systems) and 15 (Computer Resources) ([EverySpec](https://everyspec.com/MIL-HDBK/MIL-HDBK-0500-0599/MIL-HDBK-516C_52120/)). The AIAA 2025–26 carrier strike-fighter RFP and its winning reports are a useful format template ([Aerospace America](https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/institute/2026-aiaa-design-competition-winners-announced/)).

**Gate 1 exit:**
- The requirement set closes at K_we = 1.1 below a team-chosen weight cap (suggested 30–35 t).
- Every requirement traces to an analysis.
- The weight growth factor is reported.

If closure fails, relaxations are applied in the ranked order 4 g → dash-only → 300 km Mach 3 radius → 28 km ceiling, and each relaxation is recorded as a decision.

### Phase 2, months 5–11: generate, analyse and down-select concepts

The team builds three to five OpenVSP candidates. OpenVSP is at 3.51.3 as of August 2026, per port summaries ([FreeBSD](https://reviews-dev.freebsd.org/R11:ba6550b37cb3656a59a266977d68dddd16701dd2)). Suggested candidates:

- A chined cranked arrow with 70° inboard and 55° outboard sweep and canted twin tails.
- A chined 55–60° trapezoid with canted twin tails.
- A high-risk tailless variant with yaw thrust vectoring.

Each candidate is crossed with twin vs single engine and ventral vs side inlets. Each runs the same deck:

- **Geometry and drag.** CompGeom wetted area and volume. A parasite-drag build-up with C_f = 0.455/[(log₁₀Re)^2.58(1 + 0.144M²)^0.65] [T]. OpenVSP's area-rule wave-drag tool, available since 3.7.0 ([OpenVSP](https://openvsp.org/blogs/?p=435)), cross-checked with Harris's public-domain D2500 code ([PDAS](https://www.pdas.com/wavedrag.html)).
- **Lift and stability.** VSPAERO vortex-lattice runs for C_Lα, K and aerodynamic-centre travel against Mach. Only the VLM handles supersonic flow ([ICAS 2022](https://www.icas.org/ICAS_ARCHIVE/ICAS2022/data/papers/ICAS2022_0858_paper.pdf)). Digital DATCOM for derivatives, noting that it cannot model inlets or rudders ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Stability_and_Control_Digital_DATCOM)).
- **Planform rules.** The Mach-angle rule μ = arcsin(1/M) and the Jones lower bound on drag due to lift, C_D,L = C_L²S[1/(πb²) + β²/(2πl²)] [T]. The bound shows that lifting length beats span at Mach 3.
- **Inlet.** A 2-D oblique-shock inlet chain checked against the 0.809 recovery target, with capture area sized at Mach 3.
- **Engine.** First pyCycle 4.4.0 decks ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/om-pycycle/)) for P2 (bleed-bypass approximation) and a D-30F6-like high-throttle-ratio turbofan, extended from the examples, which stop at Mach 1.6–1.8 ([pyCycle example](https://github.com/OpenMDAO/pyCycle/blob/master/example_cycles/afterburning_turbojet.py)).
- **Weights.** Class II group weights with material factors, and LO coating carried as its own group. This gives the **first bottom-up K_we estimate**.
- **Internal volume.** Room for 8–17 t of fuel, about 10–21 m³ at about 0.8 kg/L, plus bays, cockpit and gear.
- **Signatures.** POFACETS 4.5 X/S-band frontal and bistatic polars ([MathWorks](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/50602-pofacets4-5)), plus the Phase 1 IR skin model with a first-order plume.

**Gate 2 exit:**
- The selected concept closes with at least 10% fuel-volume margin.
- Its bottom-up K_we is ≤ 1.1.
- Its PO frontal RCS meets the screening target.
- Its inlet meets MIL-E-5007D recovery in the shock-chain calculation.
- No conflict in the Mach 3 vs LO matrix is unresolved.

The outputs are the down-select report, a three-view, the inboard profile and the functional allocation.

### Phase 3, months 11–18: freeze the configuration with CFD and signature maps

The team lofts the OML, either in OpenVSP with STEP export or in script-based CAD (CadQuery 2.8.0, build123d 0.13.0 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/build123d/))), and iterates the area distribution. SU2 8.5.0 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU2_code)) runs Euler at Mach 0.9, 1.2, 1.6, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0 and 3.2, and RANS with SST for high-α subsonic flow, supercruise and Mach 3 cruise. OpenFOAM 13 ([OpenFOAM](https://openfoam.org/release/13/)) is the fallback, and meshing uses Gmsh 4.15.2 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/gmsh/)). Validation uses public flight and tunnel data: XB-70 drag polars and maximum L/D from Mach 0.72 to 3.07 ([NASA TM X-3532](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19770017156/downloads/19770017156.pdf)) and the YF-12 inlet measurements ([NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760012064)). The inlet needs dedicated CFD for ramp schedule, throat bleed (a NASA Mach 2 design reached about 95% recovery with about 5% bleed ([Slater, NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220009243/downloads/Slater_ISABE_2022_Conf%20Paper-v4_Final.pdf))) and unstart margin. Nozzle and afterbody get the same treatment. The aero database is tabulated in Mach, α, β and control deflection.

The pyCycle deck is extended from Mach 0 to 3.2 with the MIL recovery schedule and installation losses, T_installed = F(1 − φ_inlet − φ_nozzle). The mission is re-sized with Dymos trajectories. Trim and static margin are checked from Mach 0.3 to 3.2 with a fuel-transfer CG schedule. A transient conduction model in CalculiX or Code_Aster applies the convective-radiative boundary condition over the mission profile, including the deceleration case, where skin cools faster than spars. RCS work combines PO on the loft with edge corrections. Full-wave coupons (Bempp-cl ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/bempp-cl/)), or openEMS, unverified) cover inlet lips, serrations and absorber stacks designed with quarter-wave d = λ/(4√ε_r) logic. A coarse whole-aircraft VHF run is tractable at about 9 wavelengths. IR radiance maps are produced by band and aspect, using I = ε·M_band·A/π.

**Gate 3 exit:**
- CFD Mach 3 L/D is within 10% of the sizing value, or the aircraft is re-sized.
- The aircraft trims across the envelope with achievable CG travel.
- RCS and IR budgets are met.
- Closure is re-run on the CFD and engine decks.

### Phase 4, months 18–28: preliminary design to a PDR package

**Structure.** The team lays out frames, spars, ribs, longerons, bay and inlet structure and engine mounts, with material zoning. Loads come from the V-n envelope and gusts at an ultimate factor of 1.5, plus thermal stress σ = EαΔT/(1−ν). A pyNastran 1.4.1 deck ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyNastran/)) runs in MYSTRAN ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/mystran/)) for static, modal and buckling cases, and in CalculiX or Code_Aster for coupled thermal stress, with temperature-dependent MMPDS allowables. Hot-condition panel flutter is a known tool gap: no open Nastran SOL 145 equivalent was verified, so the team should plan piston-theory checks. The YF-12 thermal-loads program, with flight data, IR-lamp ground tests and NASTRAN predictions on the same airframe, is the validation case to reproduce ([Jenkins & Quinn, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19960027893/downloads/19960027893.pdf)).

**Subsystems.** Fuel tank layout must satisfy the heat-sink budget Q = ṁc_pΔT at the end of the dash. The PTMS/ECS rejects heat to fuel. Electrical power is 270 VDC, actuation uses EHAs, and the design covers landing gear and nitrogen inerting.

**Flight dynamics and handling.** The team builds a JSBSim 1.3.3 model ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/jsbsim/)) and trims and linearises it on a Mach–altitude grid. Control laws start as gain-scheduled pitch-rate or C* laws, then move to NDI or INDI with pseudo-inverse or quadratic-programming control allocation. Handling qualities are checked by lower-order equivalent systems, CAP (the ratio of initial pitch acceleration to steady load-factor change ([Stengel](https://stengel.mycpanel.princeton.edu/MAE331Lecture21.pdf))), bandwidth and phase delay, and Neal–Smith. A coupled inlet-unstart and CG-shift departure simulation is run. Piloted FlightGear sessions collect Cooper–Harper ratings, where Level 1 corresponds to HQR 1–3 ([NASA](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20090025299/downloads/20090025299.pdf?attachment=true)).

**Cockpit and escape.** The cockpit is laid out in CAD with canopy material, pressure-suit integration and the escape envelope. The phase also covers aperture thermal design, signature roll-ups by component, hazard-log updates and a mass-properties statement.

**Gate 4 (PDR) exit:**
- Structural margins are at least zero at 1.5 × limit plus thermal stress.
- Hot modal frequencies are acceptable.
- Level 1 handling is predicted at the grid points, with equivalent time delay checked against MIL-STD-1797 (values to verify).
- An unstart at Mach 3 is recoverable in simulation.
- The fuel heat sink exceeds the heat load at end of dash.
- No hazard remains at an unmitigated High risk code.

### Phase 5, months 28–36: the blueprint package and a virtual maket commission

The team produces model-based definition in the style of ASME Y14.41-2019 for a representative part set: a wing-box spar and rib set, the main frame at the inlet, the inlet ramp and its actuator, the canopy frame, an engine mount, and a serrated weapons-bay door. Full Y14.5 GD&T matters only if parts are to be made. The package around those parts contains:

- A general-arrangement three-view with data block.
- The inboard profile and structural-arrangement drawings.
- Systems schematics for fuel, ECS, electrical, flight control and hydraulics.
- ICDs for engine–airframe, inlet–engine and bay–store interfaces.
- A drawing tree and bill of materials.
- A weight-and-balance statement.
- The 3D master model, in FreeCAD 1.1 ([FreeCAD](https://blog.freecad.org/2026/03/25/freecad-version-1-1-released/)) or script-CAD.

The gate is a Russian-style maket commission held virtually. It checks cockpit reach and vision, ejection clearance, maintenance access and bay fit against a checklist. The final verification matrix closes by analysis, each critical technology is assigned a TRL against the Milestone B "TRL 6" yardstick, and the final report goes through a public-release check. **Gate 5 exit:** every requirement has verification evidence or a recorded waiver, and the package is internally consistent: all analyses run against the same master geometry revision.

Compute is modest except for RANS. As judgement rather than sourced data, Euler half-models of 1–5 million cells run on a 16–32-core workstation with 64–128 GB of RAM. Full-aircraft RANS with inlet and nozzle at 20–100 million cells needs 256–512 GB or cloud bursts.

## Two people can finish a paper fighter, not a flyable one

The honest feasibility verdict has three parts. **First, the digital study is feasible.** A two-person team with the toolchain above can produce a traceable requirements set, a validated sizing and mission model, a lofted OML with a CFD-backed aero database, a rubber-engine deck, PO-screened RCS polars, first-order IR maps, a thermal-structural FEM of primary structure, a JSBSim model with control laws, and a PDR-level package with selected detail parts. That is deeper than an AIAA competition report and comparable in scope to the conceptual and early-preliminary output of a real program. Its tonnages carry ±25–30% uncertainty, and its RCS results are relative, not absolute.

**Second, the evidence that makes a fighter real is out of reach.** The F-22 programme logged nearly 44,000 tunnel hours by first flight ([Lockheed Martin](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/history/f-22.html)). Each ATF Dem/Val team held a $691 million fixed-price contract ([Code One](https://www.codeonemagazine.com/article.html?item_id=88)). The KF-21 cost roughly 8.1–8.8 trillion won ([Asia Business Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025061309493127184)) and still had four core technologies refused by the US ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2015/09/27/tech-transfer-hobbles-south-korea-s-fighter-program/)). Each NGAP engine prototype contract is worth $3.5 billion ([Air & Space Forces](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/pentago-ngap-rtx-military-propulsion/)). The F-35 carries more than 8 million lines of onboard code ([Military & Aerospace Electronics](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/home/article/16715746/f35-joint-strike-fighter-benefits-from-modern-software-testing-quality-assurance)) and still could not field combat-capable TR-3 aircraft in FY2025 ([The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2026/03/25/tr-3-f-35s-delivered/)). Even the leanest stealth program on record, Have Blue, needed a government contract and about 20 months to fly two subscale demonstrators. Specific limits follow from this:

- PO codes, POFACETS included, "do not include multiple reflections, shadowing, or diffractions" ([MathWorks](https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/50602-pofacets4-5)), so no −30 dBsm claim can be validated.
- High-temperature absorbers exist only as lab coupons.
- No Mach 3 engine is obtainable.
- Engine data is out of bounds: US hot-section data is export-controlled, and Russian engines are sanctioned (status unverified).
- A CDR-level drawing set for a whole fighter is an organisational product, not a two-person one.

**Third, the physics argues against getting all three goals in full at once.** No nation has demonstrated a stealth fighter much beyond Mach 2.25, and the only Mach 3 aircraft with signature treatment treated it as secondary to speed. The defensible end-state is the A-12 compromise brought up to date: Mach 3 used as a short, high-altitude kinematic escape, and low observability concentrated where it still works (frontal X/S-band shaping, a cool supercruise plume). The best way to run the team is the one Skunk Works codified: a very simple drawing-release system, minimal reports with thorough records, and a monthly cost and schedule review projected to completion ([Libarynth](https://lib.fo.am/skunkworks); [Lockheed Martin](https://lockheedmartin.com/en-us/who-we-are/business-areas/aeronautics/skunkworks/skunk-works-origin-story.html)).

## Conclusion

The entry analysis moves the problem away from where the brief put it. Mach 3 is not the hard requirement in thrust terms: with the right engine cycle it never sets the constraint diagram. The decisive variables are the subsonic manoeuvre requirement and, above all, K_we, the unknown mass of a hot, low-observable structure. One number nobody publishes decides whether this aircraft is a 16 t fighter or a 200 t impossibility. The team's most valuable early work is therefore not a sketch but a bottom-up estimate of that number and a disciplined Gate 1 trade. The plan front-loads both for that reason.

"Invisible at Mach 3" is best treated as a mission-time signature budget rather than a property of the airframe. The A-12 programme reached the same conclusion in 1958, and the arithmetic here reproduces it: radar range shrinks only with the fourth root of RCS, IR range grows with the square root of a skin brightness that rises a thousandfold, and Mach 3 crosses a detection footprint three times faster. A team that defines success as a traceable, reproducible map of where speed, signature and weight trade, plus a PDR-level baseline sitting at a defensible point on that map, will produce something genuinely useful and publishable. A team that defines success as blueprints for a jet that meets all three goals in full is aiming at something no national program has yet achieved.
