## 1. MiG-25 (Ye-155): requirement, Mach 2.83/3.2 design decisions, structure, engines, limits, Belenko findings

### Takeaway
The MiG-25's operational speed limit of Mach 2.83 came from the engines, not the airframe. Above ~Mach 2.83 the R-15B-300 turbojets over-sped or overheated. Dashes to ~Mach 3.2 were possible but destroyed the engines. The airframe was ~80% welded nickel-steel, chosen because 1960s Soviet industry could not weld titanium reliably or affordably. The 1976 Belenko defection showed Western analysts a fast but heavy, poorly maneuverable interceptor with vacuum-tube avionics.

### Cited Findings
**Requirement and timeline**
- The program began around 1959 as a counter to the US Mach 3 XB-70 Valkyrie. The reconnaissance prototype Ye-155-R1 first flew on 6 March 1964 and the interceptor prototype (Ye-155P) followed in September 1964 — [migflug MiG-25 page](https://migflug.com/aircraft/mig-25-foxbat/) (commercial jet-flight vendor site, secondary).

**Structure and materials**
- Airframe by weight: ~80% nickel-steel alloy, ~11% aluminium, ~9% titanium. Note that the brief's "8% titanium" differs from this source's ~9% — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/mig-25-foxbat/).
- Assembly was "largely by welding, some of it by hand" — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/mig-25-foxbat/).
- Why steel rather than titanium: titanium would have handled Mach 2.8 heating better, but "welding titanium reliably was beyond their production base in the 1960s, and the metal was extraordinarily expensive." Steel was cheap, weldable and heat-tolerant — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/mig-25-foxbat/).
- Aviation Week's 1976-era archive piece described a mostly steel airframe built from "technology spinoffs" — [Aviation Week archive](https://aviationweek.com/defense/archives-mig-25-based-technology-spinoffs).
- The weight of the steel structure "necessitated its large wings" — [Wikipedia MiG-25 (via search summary)](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/MiG-25).

**Engines (Tumansky R-15B-300 turbojets, ×2)**
- Thrust per engine: 73.5 kN (16,524 lbf) dry and 100.1 kN (22,494 lbf) with afterburner — [flugzeuginfo.net](https://www.flugzeuginfo.net/acdata_php/acdata_mig25_en.php); also [Wikipedia R-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumansky_R-15). One outlier source gives 86.24 kN max / 109.76 kN afterburning — [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/MiG-25%20Fighter/1513292). Treat that as a variant or error.
- Speed limit: Mach 2.83 was the service limit. The airframe could reach ~Mach 3.2 in a dash, but "above about Mach 2.83 the compressors ran away" and the engines were destroyed — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/mig-25-foxbat/).
- "The engine tends to self-destruct when flying at Mach 3 it was clocked at," so its realistic top speed was Mach 2.8 — [Santa Clara Univ. exhibit](https://dh.scu.edu/exhibits/exhibits/show/the-cold-war--2022-/soviet-weapons/mig-25/overestimating-the-rivals) (academic student exhibit, loosely sourced).
- Speed was "limited to Mach 2.83, because runs above Mach 3.2 wrecked the engines" — [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2018/09/06/the-daring-defection-of-the-secret-soviet-super-fighter/) / [Vintage Aviation News](https://vintageaviationnews.com/warbird-articles/today-in-aviation-history-soviet-lieutenant-viktor-belenko-and-mig-25-defect-to-the-west.html).

**Weights and fuel (sources conflict, probably mixing variants P/PD/R)**
- Empty weight: 20,000 kg (44,080 lb) — [aerocorner](https://aerocorner.com/aircraft/mikoyan-mig-25-foxbat/), [aircraft.fandom](https://aircraft.fandom.com/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-25). 19,070 kg — [flugzeuginfo.net](https://www.flugzeuginfo.net/acdata_php/acdata_mig25_en.php). "15 tonnes" — [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/MiG-25%20Fighter/1513292) (likely wrong).
- Max takeoff weight: 36,720 kg loaded/MTOW — [aerocorner](https://aerocorner.com/aircraft/mikoyan-mig-25-foxbat/). 41,200 kg (90,830 lb) — [flugzeuginfo.net](https://www.flugzeuginfo.net/acdata_php/acdata_mig25_en.php). The overall published range is 36.7–41 t.
- Internal fuel: 17,700 L, about 14,100 kg — [flugzeuginfo.net](https://www.flugzeuginfo.net/acdata_php/acdata_mig25_en.php) / [aerocorner](https://aerocorner.com/aircraft/mikoyan-mig-25-foxbat/). Alternatively 17,760 L internal and up to 14.5 t total — [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/MiG-25%20Fighter/1513292).

**Belenko defection (Sept 1976, Hakodate, Japan): what Western analysis found**
- The US National Air & Space Intelligence Center (NASIC) dismantled the aircraft and found that most avionics used vacuum tubes rather than solid-state electronics — [Vintage Aviation News](https://vintageaviationnews.com/warbird-articles/today-in-aviation-history-soviet-lieutenant-viktor-belenko-and-mig-25-defect-to-the-west.html); [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2018/09/06/the-daring-defection-of-the-secret-soviet-super-fighter/).
- Analysts' proposed rationale: tubes resist EMP from nuclear attack, tolerate temperature extremes better than transistors, and are easy to replace at remote airfields. Whether this was deliberate or a result of industrial limits "remains debated" — [The Aviation Geek Club](https://theaviationgeekclub.com/a-gift-from-the-east-the-defection-of-viktor-belenko-the-soviet-mig-25-pilot-who-stole-a-foxbat-and-flew-it-to-japan/); [globalmilitary.net "Defection at 50"](https://www.globalmilitary.net/news/mig-25-defection-japan-fifty-years-foxbat-myth/).
- The radar was very high-powered, able to "burn through" jamming (single popular-press claim) — [UPI 2016](https://www.upi.com/Defense-News/2016/09/29/How-a-Soviet-pilots-defection-showed-West-the-secret-MiG/6721475152918/).
- The radar lacked look-down capability, so low-flying targets were hard to track — [SCU exhibit](https://dh.scu.edu/exhibits/exhibits/show/the-cold-war--2022-/soviet-weapons/mig-25/overestimating-the-rivals) / [warhistory.org](https://warhistory.org/2018/06/07/the-incredible-mig-25/).
- Belenko reported that the engines overheated and that the four underwing air-to-air missiles vibrated dangerously at high speed — [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2018/09/06/the-daring-defection-of-the-secret-soviet-super-fighter/).
- Analysts found the aircraft "wasn't remotely as maneuverable as they thought due to the stainless steel and low-tech nickel-steel alloy construction." It was an interceptor, not the feared air-superiority "superfighter" — [SCU exhibit](https://dh.scu.edu/exhibits/exhibits/show/the-cold-war--2022-/soviet-weapons/mig-25/overestimating-the-rivals); [warhistory.org](https://warhistory.org/2018/06/07/the-incredible-mig-25/).

### Inferences
- **Design lesson for a Mach 3 fighter:** the MiG-25's binding constraint at Mach 2.8–3.2 was the propulsion system (compressor overspeed and turbine temperature as ram-air total temperature rises), not structural heating. The MiG-25 also carried no low-observable features. A Mach 3 stealth design needs an engine and inlet cycle rated for sustained Mach 3 total temperatures, as with the J58 on the A-12/SR-71 (US, covered elsewhere).
- Steel construction carried a large weight penalty. Empty weight was ~19–20 t for a single-seat interceptor, and the fuel fraction was only ~0.34–0.38 (see table in section 7). A modern design would use titanium plus high-temperature composites and RAM, which brings new joining and heat-tolerance problems that the MiG-25 avoided with welded steel.
- The ~0.6-point gap between the Mach 2.83 limit and the Mach 3.2 maximum is a useful margin benchmark between a "never-exceed" dash and the published operational limit.

### Gaps
- **Not verified this session (fetches and searches unavailable):** R-15B-300 TSFC, wing area, g-limit, service ceiling, climb rate, range (subsonic/supersonic), time allowed at Mach 2.83, skin temperatures, welding method details (e.g., automatic/semi-automatic arc and spot welding), and service-entry and production totals. Commonly published reference values, which the writer should verify before use: wing area ~61.4 m²; g-limit ~4.5 g; service ceiling ~20.7 km; zoom-altitude record ~37.65 km (1977); service acceptance ~1972; ~1,190 built; MiG-25P range ~1,730 km subsonic and ~1,250 km supersonic. These came from the analyst's background knowledge, not from a source retrieved here.
- The exact original Soviet requirement document (Council of Ministers decree, believed to be 1962) and the A-12/SR-71 versus XB-70 framing were not confirmed beyond the migflug "counter to XB-70" statement.
- Mach 3.2 "recorded" event (popularly the Israeli radar tracks of MiG-25R overflights in 1971–73): not confirmed by any source retrieved.
- Primary declassified US exploitation reports (FTD/NASIC/CIA) could not be accessed because cia.gov was blocked.

## 2. MiG-31 / MiG-31BM / MiG-31K: changes from the MiG-25, D-30F6, Zaslon, speeds, range, crew

### Takeaway
The MiG-31 kept the MiG-25's ~Mach 2.83 limit. It swapped the turbojets for low-bypass D-30F6 turbofans of ~152 kN with afterburner each, for much better subsonic and supersonic range. It added a second crew member to operate the Zaslon passive electronically scanned array (PESA), and the BM upgrade extended detection to ~400 km against large targets. Remaining fleet estimates run from 80 to 130 of ~520 built, plus ~10–12 Kinzhal carriers (MiG-31K).

### Cited Findings
**Engines (Soloviev/Aviadvigatel D-30F6, ×2)**
- Thrust per engine: 93.1 kN dry and 151.9 kN with afterburner — [Baidu Baike MiG-31](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/MiG-31/1452676). 9,500 kgf dry (~93 kN), consistent — [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/military-products/air/fighter/mig-31-foxhound-mikoyan).
- Bypass ratio: no reliable figure was found. An informal forum figure of 0.56 is disputed by its own poster — [Key.aero forum](https://www.key.aero/forum/modern-military-aviation/98701-d-30f6-fuel-consumption). The engine is described as a low-bypass "bypass turbojet" — [Global Aircraft](https://www.globalaircraft.org/planes/mig-31_foxhound.pl).
- TSFC: 0.72 kg/(kgf·h) at max military (dry) power — [19FortyFive, June 2025](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2025/06/mig-31-foxhound-origins-history-and-technical-data-report/). The Wikipedia TSFC table lists 0.716 (dry) and 1.863 (afterburner), in lb/(lbf·h), which is numerically equal to kg/(kgf·h) — [Wikipedia TSFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust-specific_fuel_consumption). The forum also gives an afterburner SFC of ~1.9 — [Key.aero](https://www.key.aero/forum/modern-military-aviation/98701-d-30f6-fuel-consumption).

**Weights, fuel, range, speed**
- MTOW 46,200 kg. Crew of two (pilot plus weapons systems officer) — [Military History Fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Mikoyan_MiG-31); [Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/mig-31/).
- Combat radius: 720 km at Mach 2.35 and 1,450 km at Mach 0.8 — [Military History Fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Mikoyan_MiG-31); [Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/mig-31/). Conflicting figure: 1,500 km with no speed stated — [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/MiG-31/1452676).
- Range 3,300 km, combat radius ~720 km (from a lower-quality aggregator) — cited in the MiG-31BM search results via [airdatanews](https://airdatanews.com/russia-extends-mig-31-upgrade-while-lacking-a-replacement-fighter) / [Warrior Maven](https://warriormaven.com/news/russia-ukraine/upgraded-russian-mig-31-poses-new-threat-to-ukraine).
- Internal fuel: 35,550 lb (~16,130 kg) — [Planehistoria](https://planehistoria.com/mikoyan-mig-31/); same figure for the MiG-31BM — [Airfighters](https://www.airfighters.com/aircraft-data/mikoyan-gurevich-mig-31-bm). The often-quoted "16,350 kg" was not confirmed.
- Can cruise at high altitude at up to Mach 2 — [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/MiG-31/1452676). The only Mach 2.35 figure found is the condition attached to the 720 km radius.

**Radar (Zaslon family)**
- Original Zaslon: ~200 km detection against fighter-size targets, 10 targets tracked and 4 engaged — [Global Aircraft](https://www.globalaircraft.org/planes/mig-31_foxhound.pl). Designations vary (RP-31, S-800, SBI-16).
- Zaslon-M (MiG-31BM): antenna enlarged to 1.4 m diameter, 400 km detection against a 20 m² RCS target, 24 tracked and 6 engaged — [Wikipedia Zaslon (via search)](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaslon). About 280 km against a 5 m² target — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/analysis/23444).
- Zaslon-AM: 320 km, 24 tracked and 8 engaged — [19FortyFive](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2025/06/mig-31-foxhound-origins-history-and-technical-data-report/). This conflicts with the "6 engaged" figure above.

**MiG-31BM / MiG-31K and fleet**
- The BM upgrade began in 2008 with a new radar, modern avionics and the R-37M missile (~400 km range) — [airdatanews](https://airdatanews.com/russia-extends-mig-31-upgrade-while-lacking-a-replacement-fighter); [AIN 2012](https://ainonline.com/aviation-news/defense/2012-02-24/russian-air-force-describes-mig-31-upgrade).
- Production was about 520 aircraft, of which about 130 were chosen for upgrade — [airdatanews](https://airdatanews.com/russia-extends-mig-31-upgrade-while-lacking-a-replacement-fighter). Other estimates put the fleet at 80–120, with ~10 MiG-31Ks; the MoD did not disclose upgrade numbers — [airdatanews 2024](https://www.airdatanews.com/russian-air-force-receives-first-upgraded-mig-31bm-fighter-in-2024/).
- Kinzhal-capable MiG-31K: ~10–12, with the IISS Military Balance listing 12 — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/analysis/23444) / [Warrior Maven](https://warriormaven.com/news/russia-ukraine/upgraded-russian-mig-31-poses-new-threat-to-ukraine). Kinzhal is claimed at >Mach 10 and up to 2,000 km range from a MiG-31 **[RU claim, unverified; single weaker source]**.

### Inferences
- **Benchmark for "fast with range":** the MiG-31 shows that a low-bypass afterburning turbofan (dry TSFC ~0.72) gives far better subsonic and supersonic-cruise range than the MiG-25's turbojet. Top speed was still capped at ~2.83 by the same thermal and inlet limits. A Mach 3 design needs either a variable-cycle or turbine-based combined-cycle engine, or must accept turbojet-like economy at the top end.
- **Time at speed (derived):** a 720 km radius at Mach 2.35 (~2,500 km/h at ≥11 km altitude) means ~1,440 km flown supersonically, or roughly 35 minutes at ~Mach 2.35 per sortie, ignoring climb and acceleration. This gives an order-of-magnitude endurance benchmark for a Mach 2+ interceptor of ~46 t with ~16 t of fuel.
- **Two-seat rationale:** the sources only state "pilot + WSO". The usual rationale is that the WSO manages the then-novel PESA radar and data-link group intercept workload, which is consistent with the 10-track / 4-engage capability. This rationale is not directly sourced here.

### Gaps
- **Not verified this session:** structure split (commonly cited as ~49% steel, 33% aluminium, 16% titanium); first flight of the Ye-155MP (commonly cited as 16 Sept 1975) and service entry (~1981); empty weight; wing area; g-limit (~5 g); ceiling; exact D-30F6 bypass ratio. These are background knowledge only.
- No authoritative MiG-31BM or MiG-31K combat radius was found.

## 3. Sukhoi Su-27 / Su-35S / Su-57 (PAK FA) and Su-75 Checkmate

### Takeaway
The Su-57 is designed for about Mach 2, with a claimed ~Mach 1.3 supercruise. That is far below the Mach 3 target, so it is a reference for stealth integration and program pace rather than for speed. Program history: Sukhoi was selected in 2002, the first flight was on 29 Jan 2010, the first operational delivery was in Dec 2020, and there is a 76-aircraft order through 2028. Open-source estimates put the delivered fleet at only ~22–40 by 2026. Serial aircraft still fly on the interim AL-41F1 because the izdeliye 30 / AL-51F1 has not reached series production. The Su-75 Checkmate had still not flown as of the latest reports (mid/late 2026).

### Cited Findings
**Su-35S (supermaneuverability, thrust vectoring)**
- Engines: 2 × Saturn AL-41F1S (izdeliye 117S), 142 kN (32,000 lb) with afterburner and 86.3 kN (19,400 lb) dry — [migflug Su-35](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-35-flanker-e/); [weaponspecs Su-35S](https://www.weaponspecs.com/systems/sukhoi/su-35s/).
- Thrust vectoring: axisymmetric nozzles each deflect in a single plane, with the rotation axes canted. Differential deflection therefore gives yaw and roll as well as pitch. The ±15° deflection figure was not confirmed — [Wikipedia Saturn AL-41 (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_AL-41); [militarymachine](https://militarymachine.com/sukhoi-su-35).
- Internal fuel: 11,500 litres (not kg) — [weaponspecs](https://www.weaponspecs.com/systems/sukhoi/su-35s/) / [migflug Su-35](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-35-flanker-e/).
- The newer izdeliye 177S engine is claimed to match 117S thrust with better fuel economy **[RU claim, Rostec]** — [Wikipedia Saturn AL-41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_AL-41).

**Su-57: program timeline**
- Grew out of the PAK FA program, Russia's answer to the F-22. Sukhoi was selected over Mikoyan in 2002 and the prototype was designated T-50 — [migflug Su-57](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/).
- First flight: 29 January 2010 — [migflug Su-57](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/); [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/sukhoi-su-57-guide/).
- The first serial aircraft crashed in Dec 2019 before delivery. The first operational Su-57 reached the VKS in Dec 2020 — [migflug Su-57](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/). Another source says serial deliveries began in 2022 — [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/sukhoi-su-57-guide/).
- Development was slowed by structural-fatigue problems, India's withdrawal (2018) and Western sanctions — [migflug Su-57](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/).
- There is a contract for 76 Su-57s through 2028 — [19FortyFive, Aug 2026](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/08/russia-wanted-150-su-57-felons-stealth-fighters-sixteen-years-later-it-has-only-a-few-dozen/); [Defence Security Asia](https://defencesecurityasia.com/en/russia-su-57-fighter-aircraft-production-export-airpower-strategic-deterrence/). The headline "Russia wanted 150 Su-57s; sixteen years later it has only a few dozen" comes from [19FortyFive, Aug 2026](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/08/russia-wanted-150-su-57-felons-stealth-fighters-sixteen-years-later-it-has-only-a-few-dozen/).

**Su-57: production status, 2026**
- UAC delivered a batch with updated weapons in Feb 2026 without giving numbers. UAC announced another batch on 24 Sept 2026, which Ukraine's Militarnyi put at 2 aircraft, the second 2026 delivery — [migflug afterburner](https://migflug.com/afterburner/su-57-second-batch-2026-uac-delivery-russian-aerospace-forces/) **[UAC announcement; numbers withheld]**.
- Fleet estimates: ~30 production aircraft by mid-2026, or 22–40 serial airframes across open-source estimates — [19FortyFive](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/08/russia-wanted-150-su-57-felons-stealth-fighters-sixteen-years-later-it-has-only-a-few-dozen/); [Defence Security Asia](https://defencesecurityasia.com/en/russia-su-57-fighter-aircraft-production-export-airpower-strategic-deterrence/).
- Two-seat Su-57D: 40-minute first flight at Zhukovsky on 19 May 2026 — [migflug afterburner](https://migflug.com/afterburner/su-57-second-batch-2026-uac-delivery-russian-aerospace-forces/) (from the same search set).
- Export: TWZ reports that "Russia claims first foreign delivery has already occurred" **[RU claim, customer not confirmed here]** — [The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/air/su-57-with-new-upgrade-options-russia-claims-first-foreign-delivery-has-already-occurred).

**Su-57: engines**
- Izdeliye 30 / AL-51F1 has been slow to reach series production, so serial aircraft continue on the AL-41F1 (izdeliye 117). Claims that it is fitted to delivered serial Su-57s are disputed — [Defense Mirror](https://defensemirror.com/news/36775/Su_57_Jet_Deliveries_to_Russian_Air_Force_with_Second_Stage_in_Near_Future); [flyajetfighter.com](https://www.flyajetfighter.com/moscow-strengthens-the-su-57-with-next-generation-propulsion/).
- A prototype flew with the new izdeliye 177 engine in December 2025, which starts a test phase rather than a fleet re-engining — [flyajetfighter.com](https://www.flyajetfighter.com/moscow-strengthens-the-su-57-with-next-generation-propulsion/).

**Su-57: specifications (contested; most official figures unpublished)**
- Wing area ~78.8 m², including the large leading-edge root extensions. One sheet gives ~75 m² (807 ft²) — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/); [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/military-products/air/fighter/su-57); [globalmilitary.net](https://www.globalmilitary.net/aircraft/su-57-felon/); [thedefensewatch](https://thedefensewatch.com/product/su-57e-fighter-jet/).
- Empty weight ~18,500 kg (a manufacturer statement, not a weighed figure) — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/). 18,000 kg in an older sheet — [Infinite Flight community](https://community.infiniteflight.com/t/sukhoi-t50-su-57/210489).
- Internal fuel ~9,700 kg (estimate), ~12,900 kg with two 2,000 L drop tanks — [migflug](https://migflug.com/aircraft/su-57-felon/).
- Max speed: Mach 2 at altitude and ~Mach 1.1 at sea level. Supercruise ~Mach 1.3 **[RU claim, unverified]** — [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/military-products/air/fighter/su-57).
- Range: ~3,500 km subsonic, ~1,500 km supersonic, ~4,500 km ferry with external tanks — [Army Recognition](https://www.armyrecognition.com/military-products/air/fighter/su-57). [globalmilitary.net](https://www.globalmilitary.net/aircraft/su-57-felon/) gives 4,500 km as "operational", which appears to conflate ferry and combat range.

**Su-57: stealth and the RCS controversy**
- Indian Defense News, citing a French defence portal, reports that Sukhoi's own documentation places frontal RCS at 0.1–1 m², compared with Dassault's own estimate of 0.06 m² for the Rafale ("on par with or greater than Rafale") **[reported, not verified]** — [Indian Defense News, May 2026](https://www.indiandefensenews.in/2026/05/su-57-stealth-jets-rcs-on-par-with-or.html).
- Critiques: intakes are shallower and straighter than on the F-22, F-35 or J-20, with radar blockers instead of serpentine ducts, and the IRST and DIRCM turrets protrude — [19FortyFive, Feb 2026](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/02/russias-su-57-felon-has-a-big-problem-it-might-not-be-a-real-stealth-fighter-like-the-f-22-or-f-35/); [National Security Journal](https://nationalsecurityjournal.org/i-study-stealth-fighters-for-a-living-russias-su-57-felon-is-no-match-for-the-f-35/).
- India left the FGFA program in 2018 over stealth performance, cost and workshare. Analysts called its ~$25 bn cost poor value. UAC's head said FGFA was "not cancelled" — [Wikipedia HAL FGFA (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi/HAL_FGFA); [EurAsian Times](https://www.eurasiantimes.com/f-22-raptor-shot-down-russian-su-57/) (opinion-heavy); [Indian Defense News, Sept 2026](https://www.indiandefensenews.in/2026/09/indias-sukhoi-su-57-rejection-sparks.html).

**Su-75 Checkmate (LTS), status as of 2026**
- Not yet flown. UAC chief Vadim Badekha said in early June 2026 that work had reached prototype construction at KnAAZ (Komsomolsk-on-Amur) **[RU official claim]** — [migflug afterburner](https://migflug.com/afterburner/russia-su-75-checkmate-prototype-construction-2026/); [EurAsian Times](https://www.eurasiantimes.com/russias-su-75-checkmate-prototype/); [Militarnyi](https://militarnyi.com/en/news/russia-production-su-75-checkmate-prototype/).
- The first flight has slipped from 2023 to 2024, then 2025, and is now projected for late 2026. A Sukhoi test pilot said in Nov 2025 that it would fly in 2026 — [Defence Security Asia](https://defencesecurityasia.com/en/russia-su-75-checkmate-first-flight-2026-light-stealth-fighter/); [Meta-Defense, 2 June 2026](https://meta-defense.fr/en/2026/06/02/su-75-checkmate-2026-exports/).

- Designer claims: MTOW 26 t, payload up to 7,400 kg. Top speed is quoted as Mach 1.8 by one aggregator and Mach 2 by another **[RU claim, unverified]** — [Wikipedia Su-75 (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-75_Checkmate).

### Inferences
- **Stealth plus speed tension:** the only Russian LO fighter in service (Su-57) targets Mach 2 and ~Mach 1.3 supercruise. Its RCS is disputed at 0.1–1 m² class (reported), compared with ~0.06 m² claimed for the non-stealth Rafale. Inlets and engine-face masking are the most criticized areas. For a Mach 3 LO design, the inlet (shock system, plus serpentine duct or blocker under high total temperature) is the hardest conflict between speed and stealth.
- **Program pace benchmark:** from Sukhoi selection (2002) to first operational delivery (Dec 2020) took ~18 years, and from first flight to first operational delivery ~11 years. Production stands at ~22–40 airframes in ~6 years against a 76-aircraft order. The second-stage engine is still not in series 8.8+ years after its first flight (Dec 2017, background knowledge, unverified here).

### Gaps
- **Not verified this session:** Su-27 history (T-10 first flight 1977, T-10S redesign) and specs; Su-35S first flight and production totals; ±15° TVC; Su-57 MTOW; AL-41F1 thrust (commonly ~147 kN with afterburner) and izdeliye 30 thrust (commonly ~176 kN with afterburner, first flight Dec 2017); N036 Byelka architecture (nose X-band AESA, side-looking arrays, L-band arrays in the wing leading edges; T/R module count); internal bay dimensions (two tandem main bays plus small side bays); g-limit; ceiling; PAK FA requirement issue date (1999–2002). ## 4. MiG 1.44 / 1.42 (MFI) and MiG-41 / PAK DP

### Takeaway
The MiG 1.44 flew only twice (29 Feb and 27 Apr 2000) after the MFI program had effectively been cancelled for cost reasons (1997). It used ~175–176 kN AL-41F engines, giving a thrust-to-weight ratio near 1.0 at the published MTOW. The MiG-41 / PAK DP "Mach 4+" interceptor has no publicly observed prototype or mock-up as of Sept 2026, and its performance claims are unverified.

### Cited Findings
**MiG 1.44 / MFI**
- First flight on 29 February 2000 with test pilot Vladimir Gorbunov. A second, 22-minute flight followed on 27 April 2000, with no flights reported after that — [Wikipedia Project 1.44 (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_Project_1.44); [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/node/158466).
- Cancellation: the government cancelled MFI in 1997 over unit cost — [aerospaceweb](https://aerospaceweb.org/aircraft/fighter/mfi). Another account says it was shelved after brief testing in 1994 and cancelled in 1997 — [Vintage Aviation News](https://vintageaviationnews.com/warbird-articles/grounded-dreams/mikoyan-mig-1-44.html). Work ceased in 2000 — [Wikipedia (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_Project_1.44).
- AL-41F: a variable-bypass afterburning turbofan designed for supercruise. The first prototype engine flew on a MiG-25 testbed. Thrust was ~175 kN (17,840 kgf) to 176 kN with afterburner, and about 28 were built — [Wikipedia Saturn AL-41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn_AL-41); [afterburner.com.pl](https://afterburner.com.pl/mikoyan-project-1-44-миг-1-44-мфи/).
- Weights: 18,000 kg empty and 35,000 kg MTOW in one source. A Russian-language sheet gives 23,500 kg MTOW, and final specifications "remain shrouded in mystery" — [Military History Fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Mikoyan_Project_1.44); [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/node/158466).

**MiG-41 / PAK DP**
- Rostec announced the program in 2021. A retired Russian Air Force commander claimed in Sept 2025 that the external design was finalized and a first flight could come "within a few years" **[RU claim, unverified]** — [Wikipedia PAK DP (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_PAK_DP).
- Claims: speed above Mach 4 (Mach 4.3 in some reports) and the ability to intercept hypersonic missiles **[RU claim, unverified]** — [19FortyFive, Apr 2026](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/04/russia-claims-mig-41-stealth-fighter-can-intercept-hypersonic-missiles-from-the-air-nobody-can-confirm-the-aircraft-actually-exists/); [National Security Journal](https://nationalsecurityjournal.org/mig-41-russias-struggle-to-engineer-a-mach-4-3-fighter-looks-like-a-failure-now/).
- Status: as of Sept 2026 there is "no proof that development has already begun", and neither prototypes nor mock-ups have been observed — [Wikipedia PAK DP (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan_PAK_DP). Analysts judge the odds of even a few MiG-41s before 2030 to be low and treat it as signaling — [19FortyFive, Sept 2026](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/09/russias-new-mach-4-mig-41-is-the-fighter-time-forgot-and-will-never-be-the-equal-of-the-us-air-forces-f-22-f-35-or-f-47-ngad/). A 2025 article described it as "restarted" — [Army Recognition 2025](https://armyrecognition.com/news/aerospace-news/2025/russia-restarts-mig-41-program-to-intercept-the-american-f-35-in-coming-years).
- An expected 2028 introduction date still appears in a Wikipedia list but is stale — [Wikipedia list of MiG aircraft (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mikoyan_and_MiG_aircraft).

### Inferences
- No Russian program has demonstrated a stealthy aircraft faster than Mach 2. The MiG-25/31 line remains the only verified Russian Mach 2.8+ benchmark. Treat the MiG-41's Mach 4+ claim as aspiration, not a benchmark.
- The MiG 1.44 shows that a supercruise-oriented variable-bypass engine (AL-41F) existed by about 2000. The program was killed by funding, not technology. This is relevant if the Mach 3 design assumes a variable-cycle engine.

### Gaps
- MiG 1.42 versus 1.44 distinction (1.42 was the intended production configuration and 1.44 the demonstrator) was not covered by retrieved sources.
- No MFI design-speed or supercruise figures were retrieved.

## 5. How Sukhoi and MiG (UAC/Rostec) run design: OKB phases and the roles of TsAGI, CIAM, VIAM, LII, state trials

No source on this question could be retrieved before the search budget ran out. This section is almost entirely gaps. ### Inferences
- Testing a new engine on an existing high-speed airframe de-risked the MFI propulsion (AL-41F on the MiG-25). A small team doing a Mach 3 study could use the same idea digitally, by validating the engine and inlet model against MiG-25/31 and SR-71 data before integrating it into the new airframe.

## 6. KAI: T-50 Golden Eagle (Lockheed Martin partnership model) and KF-21 Boramae (KF-X)

### Takeaway
KAI built its fighter capability in two steps. The T-50 (1997–2002) was co-developed with Lockheed Martin (13% stake, F-16-derived design heritage). The KF-21 was then developed largely domestically: the EMD contract was signed on 28 Dec 2015, the first flight was in July 2022, ~1,600 test sorties with 6 prototypes ended on 13 Jan 2026, and the first production aircraft was delivered on 22 Sept 2026. The program cost was ~8.1–8.8 trillion won, Indonesia's share was cut from 1.6 trillion to 600 billion won, and the US refused four key technologies, so Korea built its own AESA radar. The KF-21 is a ~Mach 1.8, 25.6 t MTOW, 4.5-generation design. Stealth with an internal bay is deferred to the unfunded "KF-21EX / Block III".

### Cited Findings
**T-50 Golden Eagle (technology-transfer model)**
- Funding: 70% South Korean government, 17% KAI, 13% Lockheed Martin. Lockheed joined as design partner in 1997 with its 13% stake — [Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/projects/t-50/); [Military History Fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle); [Wikipedia (via search)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle).
- The KTX-2 parent program began in 1992 and was suspended in 1995 for budget reasons. Full-scale development started around 1997 and the basic design was set by 1999 — [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/T-50%20%EA%B3%A8%EB%93%A0%EC%9D%B4%EA%B8%80); [migflug T-50](https://migflug.com/aircraft/kai-t-50-golden-eagle/).
- First flight: 20 August 2002 — [European Airshows](https://www.europeanairshows.co.uk/aviation-anniversaries/august/kai-t-50-golden-eagle-first-flight).
- Cost: 2.1 trillion won for development plus 82 T-50/TA-50 production aircraft — [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/T-50%20%EA%B3%A8%EB%93%A0%EC%9D%B4%EA%B8%80). Initial program cost of ~$1.5 bn — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle) (AI-generated encyclopedia, low reliability).
- Lockheed contributed F-16-derived aerodynamics, flight control and systems experience. The F404-GE-102 engine and the wing planform are the most visible signs of that heritage — [migflug T-50](https://migflug.com/aircraft/kai-t-50-golden-eagle/); [fastestplanes](https://fastestplanes.com/aircraft/kai-t-50-golden-eagle/).

**KF-21: contract, partner and technology transfer**
- Lockheed Martin was KAI's partner when KAI won the KF-X selection in March 2015 — [Defense News, Mar 2015](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2015/03/30/s-korea-opts-for-kai-lockheed-in-fighter-deal/).
- The US refused four core technologies in April 2015: AESA radar, EO targeting pod (EOTGP), IRST, and RF jammer. DAPA said it would source them elsewhere or develop them locally — [Defense News, Sept 2015](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2015/09/27/tech-transfer-hobbles-south-korea-s-fighter-program/); [Defense News, Oct 2015](https://www.defensenews.com/air/2015/10/25/seoul-debates-best-strategy-to-acquire-aesa-radar/).

- DAPA signed the development contract with KAI on 28 Dec 2015, covering 2016–2026 — [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/defense/south-korea-contracts-kai-kf-x-development); [Korea Times, Jan 2016](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/20160121/korea-starts-kf-x-fighter-development). The program was valued at ~$7.4 bn in 2016 — [Quwa](https://quwa.org/2016/01/11/kfxifx-south-korea-and-indonesias-stealth-fighter-2).

**KF-21: configuration iterations and wind tunnel**
- ADD's options were C103 (twin-engine, F-35-like, clean-sheet, favored by the Air Force) and C203 (canard). The single-engine C501 was characterized as a 4th-generation design, whereas C103 could be upgraded to 5th generation — [Korea Times, Mar 2014](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/20140303/design-of-long-delayed-kf-x-still-in-debate); [HandWiki KF-X](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Engineering:KAI_KF-X).

C109 was selected as the prototype design, dated 2018 in the source — [HandWiki KF-21](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Engineering:KAI_KF-21_Boramae); [Military History Fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/KAI_KF-21_Boramae).

### Inferences
- **Newer-entrant program model (derived timeline):** EMD contract (Dec 2015) to first flight (Jul 2022) took ~6.5 years, first flight to end of flight test (Jan 2026) ~3.5 years, and contract to first production delivery (Sept 2026) ~10.75 years. The schedule was met almost exactly to the 2016–2026 plan. Total cost was ~8.1–8.8 trillion won (~US$6–7 bn). This is a credible benchmark for a non-superpower clean-sheet 4.5-gen program. The T-50 (Lockheed 13% partner) served as the capability-building precursor ~13 years earlier.
- **Design approach:** KAI deliberately chose a stealth-shaped but externally armed 4.5-gen first block, with an internal bay deferred. Rapid configuration iteration (C103 to C109, ~6-month model cycle, ~13,000 planned wind-tunnel hours) and indigenous development of the denied technologies (AESA) kept the schedule. For a Mach 3 LO study, plan the configuration-freeze loop explicitly. KF-X needed ~2.5 years and 4+ wind-tunnel configurations before the design froze.

### Gaps
- Rollout date (commonly 9 April 2021) was not confirmed by retrieved sources. First production lot sizes and contract values (commonly a first lot of 20 in 2024 and a second lot of 20 in 2025) were also not confirmed.
- Not confirmed: KF-21 internal fuel, empty weight, wing area, range and combat radius, ceiling, g-limit; actual (not planned) wind-tunnel hours; KAI/ADD engineering team size; the 2001 KF-X announcement and early feasibility studies.

### Inferences
- **Speed benchmark (verified):** Mach 2.83 operational with ~Mach 3.2 short dashes (MiG-25) is the only verified Russian Mach-3-class data point. It was achieved with turbojets at a T/W of only ~0.5–0.56 at heavy weights, so high Mach came from low supersonic drag and a high-altitude energy climb, not from large thrust margins.
- **Fuel:** the MiG-25 and MiG-31 carried fuel fractions of only ~0.34–0.38 at takeoff. Because afterburner TSFC is ~1.9 kg/(kgf·h) for the D-30F6, time at maximum Mach is measured in tens of minutes (MiG-31: ~35 min at Mach 2.35, derived). A Mach 3 LO fighter that wants meaningful time at speed should target a higher internal fuel fraction, typical of LO designs that carry everything internally, and a better high-Mach TSFC from a variable-cycle or combined-cycle engine.
- **Stealth plus speed:** the Su-57 (LO, Mach 2) and the KF-21 (semi-LO, Mach ~1.8–1.97) show that current non-US LO or semi-LO fighters sit around Mach 2. Going from Mach 2 to Mach 3 adds heating, inlet and nozzle problems on top of the stealth problems, especially inlets and IR signature. The MiG-25's experience (engine-limited, overheating, missiles vibrating) shows where to expect trouble.
